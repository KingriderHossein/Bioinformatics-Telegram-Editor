# Editorial Policy

## Editorial objective

Make bioinformatics understandable to a technically curious general audience without turning uncertainty into certainty.

## Story selection

Favor stories that combine at least two of these qualities:

- real scientific or technical importance
- direct relevance to medicine, DNA, microbes, AI, evolution, or human biology
- a clear surprising result or scale
- strong visual or narrative potential
- practical relevance to how biological data are analyzed
- trustworthy evidence or a clearly labeled uncertainty story

Do not select a weak story only because it has an easy clickbait headline.

## Headline rules

- Lead with the real tension or result.
- Keep the headline understandable without requiring the paper title.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Do not use miracle, cure, revolution, proves, solves, replaces scientists, or similar absolute claims unless directly justified by strong evidence.
- A question headline is acceptable only if the post answers it.

## Body logic

Use this narrative order when appropriate:

1. Hook: why the reader should care.
2. What happened: study/release/resource and the main result.
3. Why it matters: practical or scientific meaning.
4. Boundary: limitation, preprint status, benchmark context, or clinical caveat.
5. Source line.

Do not write a literal translation of the abstract.
